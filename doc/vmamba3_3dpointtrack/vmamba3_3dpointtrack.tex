\title{\textbf{Flow-Conditioned Metric 3D Point Tracking}\\[2pt]
\large Optical flow $+$ metric depth $+$ a DINOv3-conditioned Vision-Mamba-3 depth refiner}
\author{Masahiro Ogawa}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We address tracking query points in \emph{metric} 3D across a monocular video, where the
quantity that ultimately governs real-world accuracy is the per-point depth. Rather than learning
tracking end-to-end, we compose two frozen, well-understood front-ends --- dense optical
flow~\cite{searaft,waft} for 2D correspondence and a monocular metric-depth
network~\cite{da3} for the third dimension --- and learn only the residual they cannot supply:
the depth along each point's (fixed) pixel ray, refined by a tiny causal Vision-Mamba-3 state-space
model~\cite{mamba3} conditioned on DINOv3~\cite{dinov3} appearance features. This isolates the
single degree of freedom that determines 3D error, while keeping the model small ($\sim$0.5\,M
parameters) and the pipeline interpretable.
On the TAPVid-3D~\cite{tapvid3d} minival benchmark, our best configuration (WAFT flow $+$
Depth-Anything-3 depth $+$ the DINOv3-conditioned refiner) attains the highest \emph{absolute}
metric accuracy of all methods evaluated under identical conditions --- mean metric-AJ $0.246$,
ahead of strong feed-forward 3D trackers --- confirming that a targeted depth-only refinement
recovers real-world 3D structure that scale-invariant training leaves on the table. To measure
this we introduce an absolute-metric evaluation protocol that scores error at fixed metre
thresholds, exposing depth quality that the standard scale-invariant leaderboard metric hides.
A companion reliability analysis --- reproduced with each competitor's own official evaluator ---
explains why several published 3D trackers collapse under a fixed monocular, single-GPU budget,
separating genuine method limitations from evaluation artefacts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Method}

\subsection{Terminology: Mamba-3 and Vision-Mamba-3}
\label{sec:vmamba3-def}
The tracker below is an application of \emph{Vision-Mamba-3}, our reformulation of the Mamba-3 state-space
model as a drop-in \emph{attention} operator for vision. Two names recur, and we use them precisely:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Mamba-3} --- the base Mamba-3~\cite{mamba3} state-space model and its structured state-space
  duality: the causal 1-D sequence operator we build on. In this note ``Mamba-3'' also labels a use of our
  operator on \emph{purely geometric} input (ray, depth-ratio, visibility --- no appearance), as in the
  v33/v44 refiners.
  \item \textbf{Vision-Mamba-3} --- our SSD-based \emph{self-} and \emph{cross-attention} operators
  (\texttt{Mamba3SelfAttention} / \texttt{Mamba3CrossAttention}), computing
  $\bm{Y}=(\bm{L}\odot\bm{C}\bm{B}^{\top})\bm{V}$ with a \emph{structured decay mask} $\bm{L}$ in place of
  softmax scores --- linear in sequence length --- applied to \emph{vision} input: learned image/appearance
  features (DINOv3) or 2-D / multi-view spatial tokens. It runs in causal (streaming, over time) or
  non-causal (within-frame / multi-view) mode. Every DINOv3-conditioned refiner here (v35 onward) is
  Vision-Mamba-3.
\end{itemize}
A module is \emph{Vision-Mamba-3} when it runs this operator on vision input, and \emph{Mamba-3} when on
geometry alone. The operator's full derivation --- SSD $\equiv$ linear attention, the self/cross forms,
2-D RoPE, and the non-causal (VSSD) variant --- is the companion note \texttt{mamba3\_attention}.

\subsection{v1--v32: prior direct-regression attempts (prehistory)}
\label{sec:prehistory}
The freeze-flow-and-refine-depth design below is the product of a long line of direct-regression trackers
(v1--v32) that mostly \emph{failed}; those failures motivate it, so we summarise them briefly. (The numbers
here are on an earlier per-clip eval protocol and are not directly comparable to the tables in
\S\ref{sec:eval}.) \textbf{v1--v17} trained a \emph{Vision-Mamba-3} tracker to regress 3D tracks end-to-end
--- from-scratch encoders, then frozen DINOv2/DINOv3 backbones, correlation-volume features and iterative
refinement, with heavy loss engineering (Smooth-L1/Huber $+$ direction-cosine, a 2D reprojection term,
cumsum-trajectory and scale-normalised losses); its within-frame/across-frame Vision-Mamba-3 architecture
and the v6 loss are detailed in \S\ref{sec:early-tracker}--\S\ref{sec:early-loss}. The recurring failure was
a \emph{trivial zero-motion collapse}: the network minimised the loss by predicting near-static tracks.
\textbf{v18--v28} attacked the metric-\emph{scale} problem head-on with a predicted per-clip scale head and a
battery of scale-invariant / path-length-normalised losses and ground-truth-scale definitions (RMS motion
vs.\ median anchor depth); trained on the full TAPVid-3D set (v19) this plateaued at ${\approx}8\%$ mean
3D-AJ. \textbf{v29--v31} scaled the backbone (DINOv3-L, $300$\,M frozen) and added a DA3-Large metric-depth
branch, but v31 collapsed again ($0.5\%$ mean 3D-AJ).

The turning point was \textbf{v32}: freeze SEA-RAFT flow and DA3 depth, and learn only a tiny ($0.4$\,M)
causal Mamba-3 that refines the 2D track with a $\Delta\mathbf{u}$ head before unprojection. It cured the
collapse but gave a clean \emph{negative} result --- the learned $\Delta\mathbf{u}$ \emph{hurt} 3D-AJ on
every subset versus the training-free SEA-RAFT$+$DA3 baseline (mean $5.2\%\!\to\!3.4\%$), because nudging
$\mathbf{u}$ across DA3 depth discontinuities flips points out of the metric threshold. That single
observation is the crux of the method: \emph{do not move the 2D track at all} --- freeze it and refine only
the depth along the fixed ray. That is v33 (\S\ref{sec:v33}) --- the first version to beat the baseline.

\subsection{v1--v32 architecture: within-frame $+$ across-frame Vision-Mamba-3 (early tracker)}
\label{sec:early-tracker}
(The VSSD self-attention and cross-SSD operators used here are derived in the companion note \texttt{mamba3\_attention}; this subsection records the early v1--v32 tracker built on them.)

%% ================================================================

A video clip is a sequence of $F$ RGB frames
$\bm{I}^{(1)},\dots,\bm{I}^{(F)}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 3}$.
The point-tracking task is to maintain a set of $N$ persistent
\emph{tracks}; for every track $n\in\{1,\dots,N\}$ and every frame
$t\in\{1,\dots,F\}$ we predict
\begin{itemize}
  \item a 3D position $\bm{p}_n^{(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^{3}$ in a
        shared world frame,
  \item a visibility / alive flag $\sigma_n^{(t)} \in [0, 1]$ —
        $\sigma_n^{(t)} \!\to\! 1$ when track $n$ is observed in
        frame $t$, $\sigma_n^{(t)} \!\to\! 0$ when it has disappeared
        (occlusion, out-of-frame, end-of-life), and
  \item a spawn-confidence $\rho_n^{(t)} \in [0, 1]$ — $\rho_n^{(t)}
        \!\to\! 1$ on the frame at which a new track is born.
\end{itemize}
Tracks must keep identity across frames so that
$\bm{p}_n^{(t)}$ and $\bm{p}_n^{(t')}$ are positions of \emph{the same
physical 3D point} at times $t$ and $t'$.

This chapter assembles two Mamba-3 attention blocks of different types:
\textbf{non-causal VSSD self-attention within each frame} (non-causal VSSD; companion note \texttt{mamba3\_attention}),
because a single image has no causal direction, and
\textbf{causal SSD cross-attention across frames} (the cross-SSD operator (companion note)),
because a track at time $t$ may depend on its history at times
$\le t$ but not on the future. Together they realize a streaming
tracker that is linear in patch count and linear in frame count.

\subsubsection{Architecture overview}
\label{sec:track-arch}

\begin{center}\small
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
\begin{tabular}{|p{2.3cm}|p{3.3cm}|p{4.4cm}|p{3.6cm}|}
\hline
\textbf{Block} & \textbf{Operator} & \textbf{Inputs} & \textbf{Output} \\ \hline
Per-frame encoder
  & VSSD self-attn (VSSD output; companion note)
  & frame patches $\bm{X}^{(t)}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times D}$
  & frame features $\bm{Y}^{(t)}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times D}$ \\ \hline
Track propagation
  & causal cross-SSD (cross-SSD output; companion note)
  & queries $\bm{Q}^{(t-1)}$ $+$ features $\bm{Y}^{(t)}$
  & updated queries $\bm{Q}^{(t)}$ \\ \hline
Position head
  & MLP
  & $\bm{Q}^{(t)}$
  & $\bm{p}^{(t)},\,\sigma^{(t)},\,\rho^{(t)}$ \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A \emph{query-conditioned bank}
$\bm{Q}^{(t)} = (\bm{q}_1^{(t)},\dots,\bm{q}_N^{(t)})^\top
\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times D}$ carries the identity of every track from
one frame to the next. Unlike a free-floating learnable bank, each
slot $n$ is \emph{tied to a specific physical point} at construction
time: given the user-supplied query
$(x_n^{q}, y_n^{q}, t_n^{q})$ — a pixel location and an anchor frame
— the initial bank state is obtained by bilinear-sampling the
encoder's finest pyramid feature at that location:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:track-bank-init}
  \bm{q}_n^{(0)} \;=\; \mathrm{BilinearSample}\!\bigl(
    \bm{Y}^{(t_n^{q})},\; \tfrac{x_n^{q}}{W}\cdot W_{\!grid},\;
                          \tfrac{y_n^{q}}{H}\cdot H_{\!grid}\bigr)
  \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{D},
\end{equation}
where $W_{\!grid}\times H_{\!grid}$ is the finest pyramid level's
spatial extent. This eliminates the slot-assignment ambiguity that
otherwise arises when free-floating slots are matched to ground-truth
tracks via Hungarian assignment: there, the matching flips between
batches and the gradient signal becomes incoherent. With
\eqref{eq:track-bank-init} the slot identity is fixed by the input,
slot $n$ always means \emph{the trajectory of physical point
$(x_n^{q}, y_n^{q}, t_n^{q})$}, and gradients accumulate coherently
per slot.

At each frame $t$:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Encode the frame: $\bm{X}^{(t)} \to \bm{Y}^{(t)}$ via the
        VSSD operator (\S\ref{sec:track-self}).
  \item Update the bank: $\bm{Q}^{(t-1)} \xrightarrow{~\text{cross-SSD over time}~} \bm{Q}^{(t)}$
        using $\bm{Y}^{(1)},\dots,\bm{Y}^{(t)}$ as keys / values, with
        a causal mask that hides future frames
        (\S\ref{sec:track-cross}).
  \item Read out 3D position, visibility, and spawn-confidence per
        track via small MLP heads (\S\ref{sec:track-heads}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Within-frame block: VSSD self-attention}
\label{sec:track-self}

Each frame $t$ goes through the same VSSD self-attention block as
the companion note, independently of the others. Concretely
with $H\!\times\!W = 504\!\times\!504$, patch $P=14$, $D=384$,
$N=64$:
\begin{align}
  \bm{X}^{(t)} &\;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{T\times D} \;=\; \mathbb{R}^{1296\times 384}
  &&\text{(frame-$t$ patch tokens)}, \\
  \bm{B}^{(t)},\bm{C}^{(t)} &\;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{T\times N}, \quad
  \bm{V}^{(t)} \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{T\times D}, \quad
  \bm{m}^{(t)} \;\in\; \mathbb{R}_{>0}^{T}, \\
  \bm{H}^{(t)} &\;=\; \bigl(\bm{B}^{(t)}\odot\bm{m}^{(t)}\bigr)^\top\bm{V}^{(t)}
  \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{N\times D}, \\
  \bm{Y}^{(t)} &\;=\; \bm{C}^{(t)}\,\bm{H}^{(t)}
  \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{T\times D} = \mathbb{R}^{1296\times 384}.
\end{align}
The choice of VSSD (non-causal) over bidirectional SSD has two
motivations specific to tracking: (i) within a single frame there is
no preferred direction in the patch grid, and (ii) the $N\!\cdot\!D
= 24\,576$-scalar global summary $\bm{H}^{(t)}$ is independent of
$T$, so high-resolution tracking (large $T$) does not blow the
within-frame budget. Compute per frame is $O(TND)$, memory is
$O(ND)$ for the summary.

\subsubsection{Across-frame block: causal cross-SSD}
\label{sec:track-cross}

The query bank reads from the frame features via the cross-SSD
operator of the cross-SSD operator (companion note), but with two crucial choices:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{(a)}] \textbf{Queries are the persistent bank.}
        Take $\bm{C}^{q,(t)} \!\in\! \mathbb{R}^{N\times N_{\!state}}$
        from $\bm{Q}^{(t-1)}$, the previous frame's bank — these are
        the \emph{tracks asking ``where am I now?''} The kv side
        comes from the new frame's features
        $\bm{Y}^{(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^{T\times D}$, projected to
        $\bm{B}^{kv,(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^{T\times N_{\!state}}$ and
        used as the value matrix in place of $\bm{V}^{kv}$. Reading
        from $T$ patches into $N$ tracks costs $O(N T N_{\!state})$
        in Variant~B or $O(T N_{\!state} D)$ for the state of
        Variant~A.
  \item[\textbf{(b)}] \textbf{The mask is causal in time, not in
        patches.} Stack the kv side over the frames seen so far,
        $\bm{Y}^{(1)},\dots,\bm{Y}^{(t)}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times D}$,
        and let the cross-mask
        $\bm{L}^{\text{cross}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times tT}$ block the
        future:
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:track-causal-mask}
          \bm{L}^{\text{cross}}_{n,\,(s, j)}
           \;=\; \mathbb{I}\bigl[s\le t\bigr]\cdot
                 \Bigl(\!\prod_{k=s+1}^{t}\alpha_k\Bigr)\,\gamma_s,
        \end{equation}
        where the second factor is the same temporal decay profile
        as the SSD mask of (companion note), but now the running index
        is over frames rather than tokens, and the cell
        $\bm{L}^{\text{cross}}_{n,(s,j)}$ is shared across all
        patches $j$ of frame $s$. The indicator
        $\mathbb{I}[s\le t]$ enforces temporal causality so a track
        at time $t$ cannot peek at frames $>t$. This is exactly the
        recipe of the cross-SSD operator (companion note) adapted from token causality to
        frame causality.
\end{enumerate}
Concretely, the per-frame update of the bank is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:track-update}
  \boxed{\;
    \bm{Q}^{(t)}
     \;=\; \bigl(\bm{L}^{\text{cross}}\odot\bm{C}^{q,(t)}\bm{B}^{kv,(1{:}t)\,\top}\bigr)\,
            \bm{V}^{kv,(1{:}t)}
     \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{N\times D},
  \;}
\end{equation}
where the $(1{:}t)$ superscript denotes vertical stacking over the
first $t$ frames. With the Mamba-3 recurrent form
(streaming recurrence; companion note) the right-hand side is computed
\emph{incrementally}: maintain a frame-level hidden state
$\bm{h}^{(t)}_{\text{track}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N\!\times\!N_{\!state}\!\times\!D}$
that is updated in place each frame, so the streaming cost is
$O(N N_{\!state} D)$ per frame — independent of clip length.

\subsubsection{Per-track read-out heads}
\label{sec:track-heads}

Each updated query $\bm{q}_n^{(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D}$ passes through
three small MLPs:
\begin{align}
  \Delta\bm{p}_n^{(t)} &\;=\; \mathrm{MLP}_{\text{xyz}}(\bm{q}_n^{(t)}) \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{3},
  & &\text{(motion since query anchor)}\\
  \sigma_n^{(t)} &\;=\; \sigma\!\bigl(\mathrm{MLP}_{\text{vis}}(\bm{q}_n^{(t)})\bigr) \;\in\; [0, 1],
  & &\text{(visibility / alive)}\\
  \rho_n^{(t)}   &\;=\; \sigma\!\bigl(\mathrm{MLP}_{\text{spawn}}(\bm{q}_n^{(t)})\bigr) \;\in\; [0, 1],
  & &\text{(spawn flag)}
\end{align}
where $\sigma$ is the sigmoid. The position head predicts
$\Delta\bm{p}_n^{(t)}$ — the motion of track $n$ at frame $t$ relative
to its own query-anchor position — rather than absolute coordinates.
A monocular model cannot recover the absolute metric scale of the
scene from RGB alone, so any absolute-position prediction is
identifiable only up to an unknown per-clip scale factor; predicting
the relative trajectory side-steps that ambiguity entirely (the
relative motion is well-defined given the camera and the trajectory
shape). At inference, the absolute prediction is recovered as
$\bm{p}_n^{(t)} = \bm{p}_n^{q} + \Delta\bm{p}_n^{(t)}$ using the query
anchor position $\bm{p}_n^{q}$ (which the TAPVid-3D evaluator provides
as the per-track GT position at the query frame).

Stacking over all $N$ tracks gives the predicted trajectory tensor
\begin{equation}
  \bigl(\Delta\bm{p}^{(t)},\,\bm{\sigma}^{(t)},\,\bm{\rho}^{(t)}\bigr)
  \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{N\times 3}\,\times\,[0,1]^{N}\,\times\,[0,1]^{N}.
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Spawn and disappear}
\label{sec:track-spawn}

With query-conditioned initialisation \eqref{eq:track-bank-init}, $N$
equals the number of user-supplied queries — there is no fixed worst-
case slot count to over-allocate, and no need for the Mask2Former-style
``no-object'' class or Hungarian-spawn matching.

\textbf{Disappear.} A track is treated as occluded at frame $t$ when
$\sigma_n^{(t)} < \tau_{\text{vis}}$ (e.g.\ $\tau_{\text{vis}}=0.3$).
Its query embedding $\bm{q}_n^{(t)}$ still updates from the
cross-attention, so the track can \emph{re-emerge} if it becomes
visible again — the visibility flag is decoupled from the query
state.

\textbf{Spawn / birth frame.} The query $(x_n^{q}, y_n^{q}, t_n^{q})$
specifies when track $n$ is \emph{seeded}; the spawn-confidence head
$\rho_n^{(t)}$ predicts when within the clip the underlying physical
point first becomes visible, which the loss
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{spawn}}$ supervises against
$\mathbb{I}[t = t_n^{\text{birth}}]$. New physical points entering the
scene after the clip's first frame are accommodated by adding a new
query at the appropriate $(x, y, t)$; the bank then grows by one slot
without retraining.

\subsubsection{Loss}
\label{sec:early-loss}

Because \eqref{eq:track-bank-init} pins slot $n$ to the query $(x_n^{q},
y_n^{q}, t_n^{q})$, there is no slot-assignment freedom — slot $n$
\emph{is} GT track $n$ by construction. No Hungarian matching is
needed. Let $\bm{p}_n^{\star,(t)}$, $\sigma_n^{\star,(t)}$ be the
ground-truth 3D position and visibility for that track at frame $t$,
and let $\bm{p}_n^{q} := \bm{p}_n^{\star,(t_n^{q})}$ be the GT 3D
position at the query anchor. The ground-truth motion is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:track-delta-star}
  \Delta\bm{p}_n^{\star,(t)} \;:=\; \bm{p}_n^{\star,(t)} - \bm{p}_n^{q},
\end{equation}
and the model's prediction $\Delta\bm{p}_n^{(t)}$ from
\S\ref{sec:track-heads} is compared against this.

\paragraph{Per-clip scale normalisation.}
Different TAPVid-3D subsets span very different metric scales
(pstudio / adt are 1--5\,m indoor scenes; drivetrack is 10--50\,m
outdoor driving). An un-normalised L1 on $\Delta\bm{p}$ would make a
drivetrack batch's gradient $\sim$10$\times$ larger than a pstudio
batch's, swamping the optimisation. Following the TAPVid-3D
evaluator's \texttt{scaling=`median'} convention, normalise both
sides by a per-clip scale
\begin{equation}\label{eq:track-scale}
  s \;=\; \mathrm{median}\!\bigl(\|\bm{p}_n^{q}\|_2 \;:\; n \in \mathcal{N}\bigr)
  \;+\; \varepsilon,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{N}$ ranges over the GT tracks in the clip and
$\varepsilon$ is a small positive constant (e.g.\ $10^{-3}$\,m). The
scaled position residual is then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:track-resid}
  \bm{r}_n^{(t)} \;:=\; \tfrac{1}{s}\bigl(\Delta\bm{p}_n^{(t)} - \Delta\bm{p}_n^{\star,(t)}\bigr).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Loss terms (v6 — Smooth-L1, magnitude, direction-cosine, 2-D reprojection).}
A plain $\ell_1$ on the scaled residual gives the optimiser the same
constant gradient $\pm 1/s$ whether the residual is 1\,cm or 1\,m. With
many TAPVid-3D queries moving only a few cm per 8-frame window,
``predict $\Delta\bm{p} \!\approx\! 0$'' is a competitive local minimum
under $\ell_1$, and the model collapses there. v6 adds three terms that
attack the zero-motion baseline and one that grounds the prediction in
pixel space:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Scale-normalised Smooth-$\ell_1$ position} (Huber with
        $\delta\!=\!1$) on visible frames:
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:track-pos-loss}
          \mathcal{L}_{\text{pos}}
            \;=\;
            \frac{1}{Z_{\text{pos}}}\sum_{n,t}
              \sigma_n^{\star,(t)}\,
              \sum_{c\in\{x,y,z\}}
              \mathrm{SmoothL1}\!\bigl(\bm{r}_n^{(t)}[c]\bigr),
        \end{equation}
        with $Z_{\text{pos}} = \sum_{n,t}\sigma_n^{\star,(t)}$. Quadratic
        near zero, linear far away $\Rightarrow$ small residuals receive
        small gradient, so the optimiser stops wasting capacity reducing
        already-small errors and focuses on the big-error tracks where
        ``predict $0$'' is most wrong.
  \item \textbf{Scaled magnitude penalty}:
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:track-mag-loss}
          \mathcal{L}_{\text{mag}}
            \;=\;
            \frac{1}{Z_{\text{pos}}}\sum_{n,t}
              \sigma_n^{\star,(t)}
              \Bigl(\tfrac{\|\Delta\bm{p}_n^{(t)}\| -
                            \|\Delta\bm{p}_n^{\star,(t)}\|}{s}\Bigr)^{2}.
        \end{equation}
        Forces the predicted motion to have the right \emph{magnitude
        ratio to scene scale}, which under a monocular camera is the
        only magnitude information geometrically recoverable.
  \item \textbf{Direction cosine}:
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:track-dir-loss}
          \mathcal{L}_{\text{dir}}
            \;=\;
            \frac{1}{Z_{\text{pos}}}\sum_{n,t}
              \sigma_n^{\star,(t)}
              \bigl(1 - \cos\!\bigl(\Delta\bm{p}_n^{(t)},
                                     \Delta\bm{p}_n^{\star,(t)}\bigr)\bigr).
        \end{equation}
        Penalises wrong-direction motion regardless of magnitude. A
        model that gets the direction right but under-shoots magnitude
        is preferred over one that predicts the right magnitude in the
        wrong direction.
  \item \textbf{2-D pixel-space reprojection.} The TAPVid-3D queries are
        natively 2-D pixel locations; supervising in pixel space gives
        the model a geometric signal that is in-distribution with the
        input image and that has \emph{no} scale ambiguity:
        \begin{align}
          \hat{\bm{p}}_n^{(t)} &= \bm{p}_n^{q} + \Delta\bm{p}_n^{(t)},
          \quad
          \hat{\bm{u}}\hat{\bm{v}}_n^{(t)} = \pi\!\bigl(\hat{\bm{p}}_n^{(t)}, \bm{K}\bigr),
          \notag\\
          \mathcal{L}_{\text{reproj}}
            &= \frac{1}{Z_{\text{pos}}\,W_{\text{img}}}\sum_{n,t}
                 \sigma_n^{\star,(t)}\,
                 \sum_{c\in\{u,v\}}
                 \mathrm{SmoothL1}\!\bigl(
                   \hat{\bm{u}}\hat{\bm{v}}_n^{(t)}[c] - \bm{u}\bm{v}_n^{\star,(t)}[c]
                 \bigr),
          \label{eq:track-reproj-loss}
        \end{align}
        where $\pi(\cdot, \bm{K})$ is pinhole projection through the
        per-clip intrinsics and $W_{\text{img}}$ is the image side
        length used for training (normalises the pixel residual to
        $\sim\![0, 1]$).
  \item \textbf{Visibility BCE}, \textbf{spawn BCE}, and \textbf{temporal
        smoothness} unchanged from \S\,v5.
\end{itemize}
The total loss is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}
    = \lambda_{\text{pos}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{pos}}
    + \lambda_{\text{mag}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{mag}}
    + \lambda_{\text{dir}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{dir}}
    + \lambda_{\text{reproj}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{reproj}}
    + \lambda_{\text{vis}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{vis}}
    + \lambda_{\text{spawn}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{spawn}}
    + \lambda_{\text{smooth}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{smooth}},
\end{equation}
with default weights $(1,\,0.5,\,0.5,\,1,\,0.5,\,0.5,\,0.1)$.

The cross-attention's causal mask \eqref{eq:track-causal-mask}
ensures that the loss at frame $t$ depends only on frames $\le t$;
gradients flow back through the recurrent hidden state
$\bm{h}^{(t)}_{\text{track}}$, so the network learns a streaming
update rule, not a teacher-forced batch tracker.

\subsubsection{Why this operator pairing}
\label{sec:track-why}

VSSD self-attention and causal cross-SSD have complementary
strengths matched to the two axes of the tracking problem:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Within a frame, structure is 2-D and orderless — every patch
        can in principle inform every other. VSSD reaches that
        all-to-all coverage at $O(T)$ cost via the rank-$N$ global
        summary $\bm{H}^{(t)}$, instead of the $O(T^{2})$ that
        softmax MSA or bidirectional SSD would pay
        (the companion note's ratio: $\sim$\,$20\!\times$
        cheaper on a $504^{2}$ image).
  \item Across frames, structure is 1-D and causally ordered — frame
        $t$ depends on its history. Causal SSD with the temporal
        mask \eqref{eq:track-causal-mask} preserves that ordering
        and admits a $O(1)$-per-frame streaming recurrence
        (streaming recurrence; companion note), so the tracker's memory does not
        grow with clip length. The full $\bm{L}^{\text{cross}}$
        never needs to be materialised at inference.
\end{itemize}
The persistent query bank $\bm{Q}^{(t)}$ is the bridge: it is
updated by both blocks (VSSD-encoded features feed it through
cross-SSD), but its size $N\!\times\!D$ never depends on either the
patch count $T$ or the clip length $F$. The end-to-end forward pass
is $O(F \cdot T N D)$ in time, $O(N T_{\max} N_{\!state} D)$ in
memory if the within-frame summaries $\bm{H}^{(t)}$ are kept (the
default), or $O(N N_{\!state} D)$ if only the rolling track hidden
state is kept (streaming inference).

\subsection{Overview}
Given a video $\{I_t\}_{t=1}^{F}$ and query points $\{(\mathbf{u}_n, a_n)\}_{n=1}^{N}$ (pixel
position $\mathbf{u}_n$ at anchor frame $a_n$), the goal is a 3D track
$\hat{\mathbf X}\in\mathbb{R}^{F\times N\times 3}$ and visibility $\hat v\in\{0,1\}^{F\times N}$.
Our pipeline (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}) has a \emph{training-free} baseline --- SEA-RAFT
flow-chaining for the 2D track plus DA3-l unprojection for 3D --- and a small \emph{learned} module,
v33, that refines depth.

\subsection{2D track: SEA-RAFT flow-chaining (baseline, frozen)}
We precompute dense forward/backward flow for consecutive frames with a frozen SEA-RAFT model,
$\mathbf{f}^{\rightarrow}_t,\mathbf{f}^{\leftarrow}_t$. Each query is propagated outward from its
anchor by bilinearly sampling the flow at the current position,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{u}_{t+1} = \mathbf{u}_t + \mathcal{S}\!\left(\mathbf{f}^{\rightarrow}_t,\,\mathbf{u}_t\right),
\qquad
\mathbf{u}_{t-1} = \mathbf{u}_t + \mathcal{S}\!\left(\mathbf{f}^{\leftarrow}_{t-1},\,\mathbf{u}_t\right),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{S}$ is bilinear sampling. Visibility uses a forward--backward consistency test:
a hop is consistent iff $\lVert \mathbf{d}^{\rightarrow}+\mathbf{d}^{\leftarrow}\rVert \le
\alpha\,(\lVert\mathbf{d}^{\rightarrow}\rVert+\lVert\mathbf{d}^{\leftarrow}\rVert)+\beta$
($\alpha{=}0.05$, $\beta{=}1$\,px), and a point stays occluded once it fails. This 2D track is
strong on its own and is held \emph{fixed} for everything downstream.

\subsection{Metric depth and differentiable unprojection}
We use two Depth-Anything-3~(DA3) metric-depth models: \textbf{DA3-l} (\texttt{DA3Metric-Large},
0.35\,B) for all results through v39, and \textbf{DA3-g} (\texttt{DA3Nested-Giant-Large}, 1.4\,B)
from v40 onward (\S\ref{sec:v40}); a bare ``DA3'' refers to DA3-l.
DA3-l supplies a per-frame metric depth map $D_t$ (absolute metres). We sample it at the tracked
pixel and unproject with the pinhole intrinsics $K=(f_x,f_y,c_x,c_y)$:
\begin{equation}
z_t = \mathcal{S}(D_t,\mathbf{u}_t),\qquad
\hat{\mathbf X}_t = \Big(\tfrac{u_t-c_x}{f_x}\,z_t,\;\tfrac{v_t-c_y}{f_y}\,z_t,\;z_t\Big).
\label{eq:unproject}
\end{equation}
Sampling is done with \texttt{grid\_sample} so gradients flow to the (refined) depth; the depth
model itself is frozen. The baseline \emph{SEA-RAFT$+$DA3-l} tracker is exactly
Eq.~\eqref{eq:unproject} with $z_t$ taken directly from DA3-l.

\subsection{v33: Mamba-3 depth-along-ray refiner (the method)}
\label{sec:v33}
DA3-l metric depth is accurate in \emph{relative} structure but carries a global scale bias
(e.g.\ $\sim$40\% on far-field driving scenes). v33 learns a small temporal correction to the
depth, leaving the 2D track untouched. The key design choice: with the pixel $\mathbf{u}_t$ frozen,
the only 3D degree of freedom that preserves the 2D reprojection is the depth $z_t$ along the pixel
ray (Fig.~\ref{fig:ray}).

\paragraph{Inputs and architecture.} For each track $n$ and frame $t$ the SSM receives a $4$-D
feature: the normalised ray direction $\big(\tfrac{u-c_x}{f_x},\tfrac{v-c_y}{f_y}\big)$,
the depth ratio $z_{\text{raw}}/z_{\text{ref}}$ ($z_{\text{ref}}=$ per-clip median depth), and
the visibility flag.  The SSM \emph{does not see $z_{\text{raw}}$ directly}; it only sees the
scale-normalised ratio.  This is a deliberate training-stability choice: DA3-l depths span
$\sim$1\,m (\emph{adt}) to $\sim$20\,m (\emph{drivetrack}), and an unnormalised input would
require the same network weights to operate across a 20$\times$ dynamic range.

The SSM's role is therefore to predict a \emph{relative} correction factor, not an absolute
depth.  $z_{\text{raw}}$ bypasses the SSM entirely and is multiplied back in outside the learned
module (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}):
\begin{equation}
\Delta\log z_t = \mathrm{clamp}\big(\mathrm{head}(h_t),\,\pm 2\big),\qquad
z_t = z_{\text{raw},t}\cdot e^{\Delta\log z_t}.
\label{eq:correction}
\end{equation}
Because the SSM never sees the absolute scale, it cannot reason about it directly; any
global-scale correction it learns must be inferred indirectly from temporal and structural
patterns in the normalised input (e.g.\ the characteristic depth-change profile of a driving
scene vs.\ an indoor one).  A natural future improvement is to also feed $\log z_{\text{ref}}$
as a scalar context token, giving the SSM direct access to the clip-level scale without
sacrificing the per-point normalisation.

Zero-initialising the head means v33 starts exactly at the SEA-RAFT$+$DA3-l baseline and only
learns improvements.  The model has $\approx0.40$M trainable parameters.

\subsection{Why depth-only (and not 2D)}
An earlier variant (v32) added a learned 2D residual $\Delta\mathbf{u}$ on top of SEA-RAFT. It
\emph{degraded} accuracy: SEA-RAFT's 2D track is already near-optimal, and small nudges push the
sampling location across DA3-l depth discontinuities (object boundaries), flipping points out of the
correctness band. v33 therefore freezes the 2D track entirely and refines depth only --- the lever
that actually carries the error.

\subsection{v35: image-conditioned joint 2D--depth refiner}
\label{sec:v35}

v35 revisits the joint 2D--depth correction that failed in v32, but removes its root cause.
v32 nudged the pixel blindly; the SSM had no way to tell whether it was pushing toward or
away from a depth discontinuity.
v35 gives it that context through two new per-track inputs:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{DINOv3 appearance features} (64-D): sampled from a frozen ViT-S/16
  \emph{DINOv3}~\cite{dinov3} backbone (\texttt{dinov3-vits16-pretrain-lvd1689m}) at the current
  track position. These provide a local visual ``fingerprint'' of the tracked object, allowing the
  SSM to detect when the 2D position has drifted off the target.
  We use DINOv3 rather than DINOv2 specifically for its \emph{dense} (patch-level) features. DINOv3
  is Meta's 2025 self-supervised ViT, trained on ${\sim}1.7$B images (LVD-1689M, ${\sim}12\times$
  DINOv2's 142M) with a \emph{Gram-anchoring} loss that keeps the patch-token feature map sharp and
  spatially consistent throughout the long training schedule --- DINOv2's dense features, by
  contrast, degrade as pre-training progresses. Since we sample a single feature vector at each
  track pixel, this patch-level fidelity is exactly what matters, so DINOv3's dense maps are a
  better fit than DINOv2's here. (The encoder class in our code is still named \texttt{DINOv2Encoder}
  for legacy reasons, but it loads the DINOv3 weights above.)
\item \textbf{Local depth patch} (25 values). We read the DA3-l depth at a $5{\times}5$ grid of points
  centred on the tracked pixel $\mathbf{u}_0$: 25 points in total, each one bilinearly-interpolated
  depth value. Along each axis the five points sit at offsets
  $\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}\times(\text{image\_size}/14)$ pixels from $\mathbf{u}_0$, so the grid spacing and
  extent scale with the input size. \emph{Concretely, for our $896$-px inputs} the spacing is
  $896/14 = 64$\,px: the five offsets are $\{-128,-64,0,+64,+128\}$\,px, so the grid spans a
  $256{\times}256$-px window around the point. We then divide all 25 depths by the depth at the
  \emph{centre} point, giving 25 scale-invariant depth \emph{ratios} (the centre ratio is exactly
  $1$), and concatenate all 25 into the SSM input. Passing ratios rather than raw depths lets the SSM
  read local depth \emph{structure} --- the relative jumps that mark object boundaries --- independent
  of how far away the point is; this is the depth-discontinuity cue that v32 lacked and overshot
  without.
\end{itemize}

A second output head (also zero-initialised) predicts a 2D correction $\Delta\mathbf{u}$. Its raw
output is passed through a $\tanh$ (which limits it to $[-1,1]$) and then scaled by $2$, i.e.\
$\Delta\mathbf{u} = 2\tanh(\cdot)$, so the pixel can shift by at most $\pm 2$\,px.
Crucially, after $\Delta\mathbf{u}$ shifts the pixel,
depth is \emph{re-sampled} at the corrected position from the DA3-l depth map before
$\Delta\!\log z$ is applied.  This ensures the depth and 2D corrections remain geometrically
consistent (Fig.~\ref{fig:v35-concept}).

\paragraph{How the refiner uses multiple frames (the temporal axis).}
The figures above show what the SSM sees at a \emph{single} frame; this paragraph makes explicit what it
does \emph{across} frames, which is the whole reason for using a state-space model instead of a per-frame
MLP. \textbf{The sequence axis of the Vision-Mamba-3 SSM is time.} For one tracked point $n$, the refiner
assembles its per-frame feature vectors into an ordered sequence
$\mathbf{x}_{n,1},\mathbf{x}_{n,2},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{n,F}$ --- one vector per video frame, each holding
that frame's ray, depth-ratio, visibility, DINOv3 feature and $5{\times}5$ depth patch --- and the SSM
runs \emph{along this sequence}, carrying a hidden state $h_{n,t}$ that integrates the point's history.
The correction emitted at frame $t$, $z_{t}=z_{\text{raw},t}\,e^{\mathrm{head}(h_{n,t})}$, is therefore a
function of frames $1..t$ of that point, not of frame $t$ in isolation. Concretely, in
\texttt{depth\_refined\_tracker.py} the batch is reshaped to $(B\!\cdot\!N,\,F,\,D)$ just before the SSM,
so the frame axis $F$ is the recurrence length and each track is an \emph{independent} sequence.

Two properties bound what ``multiple frames'' buys here:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Temporal, not spatial.} Since each of the $B\!\cdot\!N$ tracks is its own sequence, points
  \emph{never exchange information}: the refiner mixes frames \emph{within} a track but never mixes tracks
  \emph{within} a frame. It uses many frames of \emph{one point}, not many points of one frame. (Adding
  the missing within-frame axis is exactly the v43 proposal, \S\ref{sec:v43}.)
  \item \textbf{Causal; window-8 in training, whole-clip at inference.} The two Vision-Mamba-3 layers are causal
  (\texttt{bidirectional\_mask=False}): $h_{n,t}$ sees only frames $\le t$, keeping the model streamable.
  Training feeds an $8$-frame window ($F{=}8$); at inference the \emph{entire clip} is fed as one
  sequence, so the state integrates the full trajectory.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Why this helps.} A single frame's DA3 depth is noisy and globally scale-biased, and a lone MLP would
have to correct each frame blind. The temporal state instead lets the refiner read how $z_{\text{raw}}$
\emph{evolves} along the track and (i) suppress per-frame depth flicker (a static surface should not
jitter in depth), and (ii) infer the clip's systematic scale factor from the characteristic depth-change
profile of the trajectory (a driving scene's monotone recession vs.\ an indoor scene's small
oscillations) --- which, since the SSM never sees absolute scale (\S\ref{sec:v33}), can \emph{only} be
recovered from this temporal pattern. The frozen ray guarantees the multi-frame reasoning only ever slides
depth along each point's fixed line of sight, so it stays geometrically consistent frame-to-frame.

\subsection{v36: bidirectional flow}
\label{sec:v36}

v36 uses the same v35 architecture; only the 2D front-end changes. It additionally runs SEA-RAFT
on the \emph{time-reversed} video and uses that flow to track the frames \emph{before} each query's
anchor --- the ``reversed-video backward pass'' (Fig.~\ref{fig:v36-flow}).
\emph{Important caveat.} A two-frame flow model's output $\mathrm{flow}(I_a,I_b)$ does not depend on
playback direction, so the reversed-video flow $\mathrm{flow}(I_t,I_{t-1})$ is \emph{identical} to
the direct backward flow the forward tracker already uses. The reversed-video pass therefore leaves
the 2D track unchanged: v36 is effectively identical to v35, and the sub-$0.003$ differences in
Tables~\ref{tab:median}--\ref{tab:abs} are run-to-run (GPU) nondeterminism, not a real effect. The
only flow-chaining scheme that genuinely differs fuses the forward and backward flow \emph{within}
each propagation step, $\mathbf{d}=\tfrac12(\mathbf{d}^{\rightarrow}-\mathbf{d}^{\leftarrow})$, which
we evaluate separately.

\subsection{v37--v39: WAFT optical-flow front-end}
\label{sec:waft}
WAFT~\cite{waft} (Warping-Alone Field Transforms, princeton-vl) is a stronger optical-flow
network than SEA-RAFT. We swap it in as the 2D front-end, keeping everything downstream identical,
and evaluate three variants (run at WAFT's $512$-px training resolution; scored by the same
pipeline as every other row):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{v37 (WAFT $+$ DA3-l)} --- WAFT flow $+$ raw DA3-l unprojection, no refiner. It beats the
    SEA-RAFT$+$DA3-l baseline on both metrics (normalised $0.117\!\to\!0.123$, absolute
    $0.147\!\to\!0.159$): a better 2D track alone helps.
  \item \textbf{v38 (WAFT $+$ DA3-l, bidir)} --- the per-hop forward$+$backward flow fusion of
    \S\ref{sec:v36}. As expected it is $\approx$ v37 ($0.126$ / $0.159$); fusion adds nothing here.
  \item \textbf{v39 (WAFT flow $+$ v35 depth refiner)} --- WAFT's 2D track fed into the v35
    DINOv3-SSM depth refiner. This combines the best 2D front-end with depth-scale correction and
    is our \textbf{best absolute-metric model}: metric-AJ $0.246$, beating the SEA-RAFT-fed v35
    ($0.234$) on all three subsets (Table~\ref{tab:abs}). As with v35, the refiner trades
    relative-structure quality for absolute-scale correctness, so v39's normalised AJ ($0.107$) sits
    below WAFT$+$DA3-l (v37, $0.123$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{v40: WAFT $+$ DA3-g metric depth (no refiner)}
\label{sec:v40}
\emph{Pipeline: WAFT flow $+$ \texttt{DA3Nested-Giant-Large} metric depth, unprojected directly (no
learned refiner). A pure depth-backbone swap on the training-free baseline.}

\paragraph{Diagnosis.}
Investigating the drivetrack far-field collapse (absolute-AJ $\approx0.005$, Table~\ref{tab:abs}) we
measured, at the ground-truth tracked pixels, the ratio of our cached metric depth to true depth. The current
backbone, \texttt{DA3Metric-Large} (0.35\,B), systematically \emph{under-predicts} drivetrack depth by
almost a factor of two, and the bias worsens with range:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
$\mathrm{median}(\hat z/z^{\ast})$ & drivetrack & adt & pstudio \\
\midrule
\texttt{DA3Metric-Large} (DA3-l, current) & $0.56$ (far $0.53$) & $0.98$ & $0.74$ \\
\texttt{DA3Nested-Giant-Large} (DA3-g, 1.4\,B) & $\mathbf{1.06}$ (far $1.06$) & $0.94$ & $0.70$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
So the drivetrack ``collapse'' is not a structure or tracking failure --- it is a metre-scale bias of the
small monocular metric-depth model. The larger \texttt{DA3Nested-Giant-Large} recovers near-perfect metric
depth on drivetrack (ratio $1.06$, including the far field), is roughly neutral on adt (both $\approx1$),
and does not fix pstudio (both $\approx0.7$ --- a separate, unsolved bias). Because the tracker consumes a
precomputed depth cache, this is a drop-in backbone swap: re-cache depth with the nested model and re-run.

\paragraph{Result (whole minival).}
The swap fixes the drivetrack collapse. As a training-free baseline (no refiner), WAFT$+$DA3-g scores
on \emph{absolute} metric-AJ: drivetrack $0.146$, adt $0.285$, pstudio $0.192$ (mean $\mathbf{0.208}$) ---
versus the same baseline on the old \texttt{DA3Metric-Large} depth (v37: drivetrack $0.005$, mean $0.159$).
The entire gain is drivetrack, where far-field error collapses from ${\sim}27$\,m to a $2.5$\,m median;
adt/pstudio are essentially unchanged (both depth models are already near-metric there). On the
scale-invariant leaderboard metric the swap barely moves the number (mean $0.093$), because median
rescaling removes exactly the global scale it corrects. The depth swap alone does not yet beat the current
best v39 ($0.246$ absolute), which carries the learned refiner --- closing that gap is v41 (below).

\paragraph{Relation to v42.}
This is what motivates deferring the world-frame v42 below: v42 recovers metric scale the hard way (learned
pose $+$ triangulation), whereas the far-field problem is dominated by a depth-model scale bias that the
bigger depth model fixes directly. Caveats: the nested model is 1.4\,B ($\sim$$4\times$ slower,
non-commercial license) and its metric scale is mildly resolution-sensitive.

\subsection{v41: DA3-g depth $+$ vmamba3 refiner}
\label{sec:v41}
v41 puts the v35 DINOv3-conditioned refiner back on top of the v40 baseline, but the refiner is
\textbf{retrained on DA3-g depth}. The v39 refiner was trained on the $0.55\times$-biased

loss $1.04$ (still over-correcting) and drops below $0.06$ within $50$ steps. We precomputed DA3-g depth
for the full $4{,}418$-clip training split and retrained the v35 refiner ($0.44$\,M params, $20$k steps),
evaluated with the WAFT front-end (the v41 analogue of v39).

\emph{raw} depths start at nearly the same error. A learned corrector can only undo error that is
\emph{predictable} from its inputs (appearance, ray, local depth patch) --- systematic bias --- not
unstructured noise. That is exactly what separates the two depths in the near field: after removing each
model's own per-clip scale, DA3-l's pstudio residual is $0.073$\,m (tight, systematic) while DA3-g's is
$0.238$\,m ($3\times$ looser). Systematic bias the refiner learns away; noise survives refinement, keeping
DA3-g's metric \emph{and} normalized scores low --- consistent with metric-correct $\Rightarrow$
normalized-correct, since DA3-g's near-field depth is simply not metric-correct, only noisier. In short,
the giant nested model trades near-field shape fidelity for the far-field metric correctness that rescues
drivetrack. One residual possibility --- that pstudio (only $3.5\%$ of training clips) is under-weighted
rather than intrinsically un-correctable --- is tested by a pstudio-weighted retrain (\S\ref{sec:v41b}).

  therefore entirely adt, DELTA's architectural stronghold, not a near-field deficit.
  \item \textbf{It lands exactly on the predicted ceiling.} v42's $0.225$ mean confirms the
  \S\ref{sec:v41b} projection empirically at full training length.
  \item \textbf{The near-field noise floor still binds.} v42's pstudio ($0.213$) sits \emph{below} the
  pstudio-only v41b ($0.225$) --- one pipeline cannot spend all its capacity on pstudio --- and far below the
  DA3-l v39 ($0.279$); pstudio median error is $0.652$\,m against DA3-l's $0.348$\,m. Oversampling extracts
  the systematic part of the error but cannot cross DA3-g's intrinsic near-range depth noise.
  \item \textbf{v39 remains the single-method ceiling.} At $0.225$ the best single DA3-g pipeline still
  trails v39's $0.246$ by $0.021$; the $+0.003$ drivetrack edge cannot offset the near-field deficit.
  Beating v39 everywhere needs either the per-domain hybrid (\S\ref{sec:v41}) or a depth backbone that is
  metric \emph{and} low-noise in the near field.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{DA3-l vs.\ DA3-g: architecture, loss, and where the near-field error really is}
\label{sec:da3lg}
The depth-backbone swap (DA3-l $\to$ DA3-g, \S\ref{sec:v40}--\S\ref{sec:v42}) helped far-field drivetrack but
not the near field, and the refiner could halve DA3-l's near depth error ($0.73\!\to\!0.35$\,m) but barely
move DA3-g's ($0.78\!\to\!0.73$\,m). This section consolidates \emph{why}, from two angles: the two models'
architecture and training (read from the DA3 code and paper~\cite{da3}), and a direct per-point error
analysis on the minival that \emph{overturns} the natural ``DA3-g depth is noisier'' explanation.

\paragraph{DA3-l --- distilled monocular depth.}
DA3-l (\texttt{da3metric-large}) is a \emph{single monocular} network: a DINOv2 ViT-L backbone with a plain
DPT depth head (one output channel). It is trained by \emph{distillation} --- a teacher trained on
\emph{synthetic} data emits dense, clean pseudo-depth --- and made metric only by a canonical-focal-length
rescale ($f^{c}/f$, $f^{c}{=}300$). Distilling a smooth synthetic teacher gives it low-variance,
temporally-stable \emph{shape and scale}; the focal rescale is a \emph{deterministic} per-frame function (no
fitting), so it introduces a global scale \emph{bias} but no temporal wobble.

\paragraph{DA3-g --- nested multi-view geometry $+$ metric fusion.}
DA3-g (\texttt{da3nested-giant-large}) is a \emph{nested} model of two branches: an ``any-view'' multi-view
geometry branch (DINOv2 ViT-g with cross-view attention and a \emph{depth-ray} DualDPT head, two channels)
\emph{plus} the DA3-l branch itself. The any-view branch is trained scale-\emph{invariant} (ground truth
normalised by the scene point map's mean $\ell_2$ norm) with a confidence-weighted $\ell_1$ depth loss
alongside ray-map, point-cloud and gradient terms, on \emph{real} multi-view reconstructions (COLMAP and
active sensors) the DA3 authors themselves call ``noisy or sparse.'' At inference
(\texttt{NestedDepthAnything3Net}, \texttt{model/da3.py}) the nested model does \emph{not} copy DA3-l's
depth; it fits a \emph{single global scalar} $s$ each frame by least squares so the any-view depth best
matches DA3-l, then sets $\text{DA3-g}=\text{any-view}\times s$.
\textbf{So DA3-g's per-pixel shape is entirely the any-view branch's; the metric branch (= DA3-l) contributes
only that one scalar --- its own clean shape is discarded.} Two asymmetries follow: (i) DA3-g's shape is
supervised by dirtier real-reconstruction geometry, not the clean synthetic teacher, so more parameters
cannot buy back cleanliness the labels lack; (ii) the single \emph{global} scalar cannot fix any
\emph{within-clip} scale variation in the any-view branch.

\paragraph{Why DA3-g's scale drifts frame to frame, though DA3-l's is stable.}
A natural objection: DA3-g's scale comes from DA3-l, and DA3-l's scale is temporally stable --- so why does
DA3-g's per-frame scale wobble $\times4.3$ more (Table~\ref{tab:da3lg-scale})? Because DA3-g does \emph{not}
inherit DA3-l's scale; it inherits a per-frame least-squares \emph{fit}
$s=\arg\min_s\lVert a-s\,b\rVert^2=\sum_i a_i b_i/\sum_i b_i^2$ (with $a=$ DA3-l depth, $b=$ any-view depth),
and the \emph{fit} --- not DA3-l --- is the noisy step. Four things make $s$ wobble even though DA3-l is steady:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Re-fit independently every frame, no temporal coupling.} DA3-l's own scale is a deterministic
  focal formula (no optimisation); the nested model \emph{adds} a per-frame fit, and any independent
  per-frame estimate carries variance --- nothing ties frame $t$'s $s$ to frame $t{-}1$'s.
  \item \textbf{It reconciles two \emph{differently-structured} depth fields.} DA3-l (smooth monocular) and
  the any-view branch (multi-view geometry) do not agree pixel-for-pixel, so the single best-fit scalar
  depends on the depth distribution and the visible-point set --- which change with scene content frame to
  frame. Same two stable models, different overlap $\Rightarrow$ different $s$.
  \item \textbf{The least-squares scalar is $b^2$-weighted, so the \emph{far field} sets it.}
  $s=\sum a_i b_i/\sum b_i^2$ is a mean of the ratio $a_i/b_i$ weighted by any-view depth \emph{squared},
  hence dominated by the largest-depth pixels (and the confidence/non-sky mask). In a near-field indoor
  scene (pstudio) the ``far'' is the sparse, occlusion-prone background --- a noisy, content-dependent
  anchor that jumps as the visible far surfaces change. This is why pstudio drifts most.
  \item \textbf{A multi-view model run single-frame.} Our cache feeds the any-view branch one frame at a
  time, so its own per-frame geometry/scale is under-constrained and more variable, injecting further noise
  into the fit.
\end{enumerate}
So DA3-l's stability sets a stable \emph{target}, but the drift is manufactured \emph{at fusion time} by a
per-frame, far-pixel-weighted least-squares fit against a wobbly multi-view branch over a changing mask ---
the fusion never copies DA3-l's scale, it re-solves for it each frame. This is exactly why a temporally
smooth scale (the offline \texttt{lprof} re-anchor) or a per-frame-correct scale (oracle) removes the drift,
and what v43's per-frame scale head (\S\ref{sec:v43}) learns to undo.

\paragraph{Where the error actually is (per-point analysis).}
We test the ``DA3-g is noisier'' story directly (\texttt{scripts/analyze\_da3lg\_shape\_crossover.py}): on
all $150$ minival clips we sample both depths at the ground-truth pixel, remove a scale to expose shape, and
measure the scale-invariant error $|\log(s\,\hat z/z^{*})|$ stratified by distance (Fig.~\ref{fig:da3lg}).
Removing the scale two ways is the crux: \emph{per-frame} (one scale per frame) isolates spatial shape;
\emph{per-clip} (one scale for the whole clip) additionally exposes temporal scale drift
(Table~\ref{tab:da3lg-scale}).

\paragraph{Conclusion --- and a correction.}
The earlier reading (\S\ref{sec:v41b}) that DA3-g carries ``intrinsic near-field \emph{noise}'' was measured
with a per-clip scale and is wrong in attribution: DA3-g's per-frame spatial shape on pstudio is as good as
DA3-l's ($0.031$ vs.\ $0.029$); what is $4.3\times$ worse is its \emph{per-frame scale}, which wobbles over
time (its per-frame depth cache re-fits the nested model's least-squares scalar independently each frame,
whereas DA3-l's focal rescale is deterministic and stable). Because removing one scalar \emph{per frame}
collapses the error, the defect is low-dimensional --- one wrong global scale per frame, shared by all
points --- and hence fixable, unlike per-pixel noise. This also explains why the per-track refiner cannot
repair it: it never sees absolute scale (\S\ref{sec:v33}) and, mixing only along time within a track, cannot
estimate a \emph{per-frame, all-points-shared} correction. \textbf{The lever is therefore per-frame scale
stabilisation of DA3-g, not a spatial depth fusion:} either (i) re-anchor DA3-g's per-frame scale to a
temporally stable reference (e.g.\ DA3-l's smooth scale profile) offline, or (ii) add a per-frame scale head
fed by \emph{within-frame} pooling (the within-frame Vision-Mamba-3 mixing of \S\ref{sec:v43}), so one scale
per frame is estimated from all points in that frame.

\paragraph{Offline validation.}
Both the diagnosis and a fix hold up without retraining (\texttt{scripts/generate\_da3g\_reanchor.py}):
we rescale DA3-g's pstudio depth by \emph{one scalar per frame} and re-evaluate with the v42 refiner
(WAFT front-end), Table~\ref{tab:da3lg-reanchor}. An \emph{oracle} per-frame scale (from GT) more than
\emph{doubles} the scale-invariant leaderboard 3D-AJ ($0.026\!\to\!0.064$) --- direct proof that per-frame
scale drift, not spatial shape, dominates pstudio's leaderboard score. A \emph{GT-free} re-anchor that gives
DA3-g the temporally stable DA3-l scale profile (preserving DA3-g's clip-average scale, so no bias is
imported) improves \emph{both} metrics with the existing refiner: normalized $0.026\!\to\!0.043$ ($+62\%$)
and absolute metric-AJ $0.213\!\to\!0.227$ (past the pstudio-only v41b, $0.225$), median error
$0.652\!\to\!0.584$\,m. The oracle's absolute metric-AJ instead dips ($0.176$) only because the refiner was
trained on un-rescaled depth --- retraining the refiner on re-anchored depth is the in-model next step.

\subsection{v43: in-model per-frame scale head}
\label{sec:v43}
\S\ref{sec:da3lg} localised DA3-g's near-field defect to a \emph{per-frame global scale} that drifts over
time; removing it offline (even by borrowing DA3-l's scale profile) lifts pstudio on both metrics. v43
learns that correction \emph{in-model}. Two axes of information matter --- across frames (time) and across
points (within a frame) --- and v43 keeps them separate.

\paragraph{Where the multiple-frame information comes from (inherited from v35).}
v43 adds \emph{no} new temporal model. It inherits v35's: as detailed in \S\ref{sec:v35} (``how the refiner
uses multiple frames''), the Vision-Mamba-3 refiner (DINOv3-conditioned, hence \emph{Vision}-Mamba-3, not
the geometry-only Mamba-3 of v33) already consumes the whole clip along the \emph{time} axis ---
for each tracked point $n$, its per-frame feature vectors form a sequence
$\mathbf{x}_{n,1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{n,F}$, the batch is reshaped to $(B\!\cdot\!N,\,F,\,D)$, and its
\emph{Vision-Mamba-3 state-space model runs causally along that frame sequence, one track at a time} (no cross-track
mixing). So every point already carries its own temporal history. That --- and nothing else --- is how v43
(and v35) uses multiple frames.

\paragraph{What v43 adds: the orthogonal, within-frame axis.}
The per-frame scale is one number \emph{shared by all points in a frame}, so it cannot be read from any single
track's temporal history --- it needs aggregation \emph{across points within a frame}, the one axis the
per-track SSM never touches. v43 adds exactly that, as a small stage after the temporal SSM:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Run the full v35 pipeline (\S\ref{sec:v35}): inputs $\to$ embed $\to$ per-track temporal Vision-Mamba-3 SSM
  $\to$ the $\Delta\log z,\ \Delta\mathbf{u}$ heads, giving per-point features
  $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times F\times N\times D}$ and a first depth $z_{t,n}$.
  \item \textbf{Within-frame pooling.} For each frame $f$ \emph{independently}, take the plain masked
  \emph{mean} of the per-point features over the visible points,
  $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_f=\big(\sum_n v_{f,n}\,\mathbf{x}_{f,n}\big)\big/\big(\sum_n v_{f,n}\big)$ with
  $v_{f,n}\in\{0,1\}$ the visibility, giving one per-frame vector
  $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_f\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times F\times D}$. The points do \emph{not} interact --- it is a flat
  average, not attention.
  \item \textbf{Per-frame scale head.} A zero-initialised MLP maps each $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_f$ to one bounded
  log-scale $\Delta s_f=s_{\max}\tanh(\mathrm{MLP}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_f))$, with $s_{\max}{=}0.5$.
  \item \textbf{Apply.} Rescale \emph{every} point in frame $f$ by that single scalar,
  $z_{t,n}\leftarrow z_{t,n}\,e^{\Delta s_f}$, then unproject. Zero-init $\Rightarrow\Delta s_f{=}0
  \Rightarrow$ v43 equals v42 at step 0.
\end{enumerate}
Because the pooled $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_f$ come \emph{out of} the temporal SSM, they are already temporally
aware; the pooling adds the \emph{spatial} aggregation, so the head can estimate the frame's true global
scale --- the one-scalar-per-frame wobble no per-track model can see. v43 warm-starts from v42 and trains on
DA3-g depth. It is a deliberately \emph{minimal} slice of \S\ref{sec:vfuture}: only a per-frame global
\emph{scale}, not a per-frame pose or per-point motion.

\paragraph{Two shortcomings of v43, and the proposed v44.}
v43 recovers only part of the offline gain (Table~\ref{tab:v43}), and its two structural limits say why.
\emph{(i) The pooling is a flat average, with no interaction between points.} A masked mean already drops the
\emph{invisible} points, so occlusion is \emph{not} the issue --- the issue is that among the \emph{visible}
points it cannot tell a reliable near-field anchor from a visible-but-noisy far or dynamic one (exactly the
points that corrupt the least-squares scale, \S\ref{sec:da3lg}), and it discards the point-to-point
arrangement that pins the true scale. Letting the $N$ points \emph{interact} first --- a \emph{bidirectional
within-frame Vision-Mamba-3 self-attention} over the point axis (reshape to $(B\!\cdot\!F,N,D)$, order-agnostic,
linear in $N$), \emph{then} pool --- turns the flat average into a learned, robust, structure-aware
aggregation. The novelty over v43 is the \emph{attention between points}, not the visibility mask (which v43
already has).
\emph{(ii) The scale is applied \emph{after} the refiner, not to the depth it consumes.} v43 rescales the
\emph{output} $z_{t,n}$, so the v35 refiner still ingests the flickering DA3-g depth --- its
$z/z_{\text{ref}}$ input drifts frame to frame --- and must work against it. It is cleaner to
\emph{de-flicker first}: a small within-frame module (Mamba-3 / MLP over the frame's points) estimates the
per-frame scale and produces a stabilised depth $z^{\text{stab}}=z_{\text{raw}}\,e^{\Delta s_f}$, and the
standard v35 refiner then runs on $z^{\text{stab}}$ --- so its along-ray $\Delta\log z$ and $\Delta\mathbf{u}$
corrections operate on temporally consistent depth instead of fighting the drift. This two-stage form,
\emph{de-flicker $\to$ v35 refine} (with the within-frame attention of (i) as the de-flicker's aggregator),
is the proposed \textbf{v44}.

\paragraph{Result.}
Trained $20$k steps warm-started from v42 (masked-mean within-frame pooling), v43 improves v42 on
\emph{both} metrics across \emph{all three} subsets, with the gain concentrated exactly where the drift
lives --- pstudio and adt --- while drift-free drivetrack is flat (Table~\ref{tab:v43}). Absolute metric-AJ
rises to a mean $0.229$, the \textbf{best of any single DA3-g pipeline} and now \emph{ahead of}
DELTA$+$DA3-g ($0.227$, Table~\ref{tab:abs-g}), though still below the DA3-l v39 ($0.246$); the
scale-invariant leaderboard mean rises $0.086\!\to\!0.092$. On pstudio the learned head recovers part of the
offline re-anchor's gain --- normalized $0.026\!\to\!0.035$ (vs.\ lprof's $0.043$), absolute
$0.213\!\to\!0.220$ (vs.\ lprof's $0.227$) --- so the mechanism is right, but masked-mean pooling leaves
headroom. Replacing it with the bidirectional within-frame Vision-Mamba-3 mixing, so the head sees
point-to-point structure rather than a mean, is the natural next lever.

\subsection{v44: DA3-g de-flicker (standalone)}
\label{sec:v44}
v43 applied its per-frame scale correction \emph{after} the v35 refiner, so the refiner still ingested the
flickering depth (\S\ref{sec:v43}). v44 fixes the ordering and factors the de-flicker out as a
\emph{standalone} method, comparable head-to-head with the refiner-free WAFT$+$DA3-g baseline (v40): its only
job is to remove DA3-g's per-frame scale drift (\S\ref{sec:da3lg}); it carries no along-ray or 2D correction.

\paragraph{Method.} The correction is a single log-scale per frame, estimated along the \emph{time} axis:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item For each frame $f$, mean-pool the visible points' geometric features
  $[\mathbf{r},\,z_{\text{raw}}/z_{\text{ref}},\,v]$ into one \emph{frame token} $\mathbf{g}_f$.
  \item A \emph{time-axis} Mamba-3 (bidirectional --- a de-flicker may look ahead) mixes the sequence
  $\mathbf{g}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{g}_{F}$ across frames, modelling the scale trajectory directly.
  \item A zero-initialised head emits $\Delta s_f=s_{\max}\tanh(\cdot)$; every point in frame $f$ is
  rescaled $z\leftarrow z_{\text{raw}}\,e^{\Delta s_f}$ and unprojected along the frozen ray.
\end{enumerate}
Zero-init $\Rightarrow$ v44 starts \emph{exactly} at WAFT$+$DA3-g (v40). It uses plain Mamba-3, not the
2D-image vmamba3 machinery of v35: the flicker is a purely temporal, per-frame-\emph{global} phenomenon, so
the model is a light time-axis sequence model over frame tokens ($\sim$0.3\,M params, no DINOv3, no depth
patch). Loss is 3D-only (2D track frozen); trained from scratch, $20$k steps, DA3-g depth. v44 is the
\emph{learned} counterpart of the \texttt{lprof} offline re-anchor (\S\ref{sec:da3lg}) --- both replace
DA3-g's per-frame scale with a temporally consistent one, but v44 learns it end-to-end from the 3D loss.

\paragraph{Result --- the standalone de-flicker does not help.}
Trained $20$k steps, v44 lands essentially \emph{at} the WAFT$+$DA3-g (v40) baseline on the scale-invariant
leaderboard metric and slightly \emph{below} it on absolute (Table~\ref{tab:v44}): mean normalized $0.093$
(v40: $0.093$), mean absolute $0.203$ (v40: $0.208$). It does \emph{not} reproduce the offline
\texttt{lprof} re-anchor's pstudio gain (normalized $0.039$ vs.\ lprof's $0.043$, \S\ref{sec:da3lg}), and it
mildly \emph{regresses} drift-free drivetrack (absolute $0.146\!\to\!0.123$). So a per-frame scale learned
from mean-pooled \emph{sparse tracked points} --- under a per-clip-normalised 3D loss --- is too weak a
signal to recover the drift that the dense offline re-anchor removes, and without any along-ray refinement
it applies scales that can hurt where there is no drift. The lesson: the de-flicker is not useful in
isolation; it needs the refiner (v45).

\subsection{v45: v44 de-flicker $+$ v35 refiner}
\label{sec:v45}
v45 is the two-stage pipeline \S\ref{sec:v43} argued for --- \textbf{de-flicker first, then refine}.
Stage~1 is v44: it produces a stabilised depth $z^{\text{stab}}=z_{\text{raw}}\,e^{\Delta s_f}$ (and the
correspondingly rescaled depth map). Stage~2 is the standard v35 refiner (\S\ref{sec:v35}), run on
$z^{\text{stab}}$ instead of raw DA3-g depth, so its along-ray $\Delta\log z$ and $\Delta\mathbf{u}$
corrections operate on temporally consistent depth. Where v43 bolts a scale onto the refiner's \emph{output}
and v44 de-flickers with \emph{no} refinement, v45 gives the refiner clean \emph{input} --- combining v44's
temporal stability with v35's per-point sharpening.

\paragraph{Result --- best DA3-g \emph{absolute} accuracy, but v43 keeps the leaderboard.}
Warm-started from v44 (stage 1) $+$ v42 (stage 2) and fine-tuned $20$k steps, v45 gives the \textbf{best
absolute metric-AJ of any single DA3-g pipeline}: mean $0.232$ (Table~\ref{tab:v45}), ahead of v43
($0.229$), v42 ($0.225$) and DELTA$+$DA3-g ($0.227$), and best per subset on drivetrack ($0.173$) and
pstudio ($0.226$, essentially matching the offline \texttt{lprof} re-anchor's $0.227$), tied with v43 on adt
($0.297$). So the two-stage \emph{de-flicker $\to$ refine} ordering --- giving the refiner temporally stable
depth --- does help the fixed-threshold metric, and unlike the standalone de-flicker (v44) it does not
regress drivetrack. \textbf{But it does not win the scale-invariant leaderboard metric}: normalized mean
$0.088$ is \emph{below} v43's $0.092$ (pstudio $0.029$ vs.\ v43's $0.035$). The post-hoc scale head (v43)
remains the better temporal-consistency fix; the two-stage pipeline (v45) is the better absolute-accuracy
fix. Neither reaches the DA3-l v39 absolute ceiling ($0.246$) --- v45 narrows the gap to $0.014$.

\subsection{v\_future: world-frame tracking with ego-motion/object-motion decomposition (proposed)}
\label{sec:vfuture}
\emph{Status: \textbf{deferred}. The first-experiment gate was run (below) and revealed that the drivetrack
far-field error is dominated by a monocular metric-depth \emph{scale} bias --- fixed directly by v40's
depth-backbone swap --- rather than by the lack of a world frame. Triangulation with correct pose is exact
(oracle error $0.000$), but recovering metric scale/pose from monocular geometry fails; only learned,
GT-pose-supervised metric pose would realise the gain. Kept here as a documented design; revisit if v40
plateaus. Built on v39 and would warm-start from it.}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
Everything up to v39 refines each point's depth \emph{independently, in per-frame camera coordinates}: the
SSM runs over time per track with no cross-track mixing. This is robust---it needs no camera pose, which is
exactly why it survives where the pose-dependent TAPIP3D collapses (\S\ref{sec:sota})---but it leaves
absolute accuracy on the table, most visibly in the \textbf{far field on drivetrack} (absolute-AJ
$\approx0.005$, Table~\ref{tab:abs}), the classic monocular metric-depth collapse: a single ray cannot
recover metre-accurate depth at $30$--$100$\,m.

\paragraph{Idea.}
Lift the tracked points into a shared \emph{world frame} and factor each point's motion into a per-frame
camera pose $T_{w\to c}(t)\in SE(3)$ \emph{shared by all points} and a per-point object-motion residual:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{X}^{\text{cam}}_t \;=\; T_{w\to c}(t)\,\bigl(\mathbf{X}^{w}_0 + \Delta^{\text{obj}}_t\bigr),
  \label{eq:worldframe}
\end{equation}
so a genuinely \emph{static} point has $\Delta^{\text{obj}}_t\equiv0$ and a constant world position
$\mathbf{X}^{w}_0$. This buys two things the per-frame formulation cannot:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A free multi-frame constraint.} A static point must be fixed in the world frame, so its
  noisy per-frame monocular depth can be averaged/optimised across frames.
  \item \textbf{Parallax triangulation.} With relative pose known, a static point seen from a moving camera
  can be \emph{triangulated}, which is far more accurate than monocular depth at range. This directly
  attacks the far-field bottleneck, correcting DA3-l's depth-scale bias rather than only refining along the
  ray (\S\ref{sec:vfuture-gate}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Architecture (what changes from v35/v39).}
Estimating a \emph{shared} per-frame pose requires aggregating information \emph{across points within a
frame}---precisely the non-causal Vision-Mamba-3 cross-attention derived in the backbone but unused by the
per-track temporal refiner. v\_future adds a \emph{within-frame} mixing stage (bidirectional Vision-Mamba-3
self-attention over the $N$ points of each frame, order-agnostic, linear in $N$) after the existing
across-frame causal Vision-Mamba-3 refiner, giving the two-axis operator \emph{within-frame non-causal $\times$ across-frame
causal}. On top of the pooled within-frame features it adds three zero-initialised heads so v\_future starts
exactly at v39: (i) a per-frame \textbf{camera-pose} head ($6$D rotation $+$ translation $\to$ identity at
init); (ii) a per-point \textbf{moving/static} logit; (iii) a per-point \textbf{depth log-variance}
(uncertainty), turning the $\ell_1$ position loss into a heteroscedastic (Laplace) NLL that down-weights
unreliable far-field depth.

\paragraph{Three supporting components.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Rigid motion grouping.} Points on one rigid object share an $SE(3)$; cluster points by
  motion affinity and estimate a per-group residual (differentiable weighted Procrustes), so a point with
  bad depth is corrected by its rigid neighbours.
  \item \textbf{Depth uncertainty.} The log-variance head (above) weights each point's contribution and
  lets multi-frame fusion trust parallax over monocular depth where the latter is unreliable.
  \item \textbf{Test-time optimisation.} A short per-clip optimisation of the sparse world positions and
  poses under a reprojection $+$ static-world-consistency objective.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Pose supervision.}
Drivetrack ships per-frame ground-truth camera extrinsics ($\texttt{extrinsics\_w2c}$), which we use to
supervise the pose head and to derive self-supervised static/dynamic targets (a static point's world
position is constant); adt and Panoptic Studio have no pose, so there the pose is self-supervised through a
reprojection $+$ robust static-point Procrustes objective.

\paragraph{Keeping it robust (avoiding TAPIP3D's failure).}
World-frame reasoning reintroduces the very pose dependence that broke TAPIP3D on this hardware. We keep
v\_future \emph{additive} on top of v39 with a confidence gate: when the confidently-static set is too small
or the scene is too rotational (egocentric ADT), v\_future falls back to the v39 per-frame estimate. Poses are
estimated \emph{robustly from confidently-static points} (RANSAC-weighted alignment), never by trusting raw
monocular depth.

\paragraph{First-experiment gate.}
\label{sec:vfuture-gate}
Before any training we test the load-bearing claim (parallax triangulation fixes far-field depth) directly
on drivetrack: using exact ground-truth pixel correspondences, compare (a) monocular DA3-depth unprojection
against (b) DLT triangulation across a widened baseline, run with both the ground-truth pose (oracle) and a
pose estimated from the static set, stratified by ground-truth depth (near/mid/far). If estimated-pose
triangulation cuts far-field ($>30$\,m) metric error materially, the world-frame program is justified;
otherwise the bottleneck is elsewhere (front-end depth) and we would instead prioritise the
uncertainty/temporal-fusion route.

\subsection{Training}
Only the $0.40$M-parameter refiner is trained. The loss is 3D-position-only (the 2D track and
visibility are frozen, so they carry no gradient):
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{V}|}\sum_{(t,n)\in\mathcal{V}}
\frac{\lVert \hat{\mathbf X}_{t,n}-\mathbf X^{*}_{t,n}\rVert_1}{s_{\text{clip}}},
\end{equation}
an $\ell_1$ error over visible points, normalised by the per-clip median anchor-frame depth
$s_{\text{clip}}$ (matching the benchmark's scale convention). We train $20$k steps (AdamW,
lr $3\!\times\!10^{-4}$, warmup $500$, $8$-frame windows, bf16; $\sim$9.5\,h on one GPU).

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Protocol and metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

\paragraph{Dataset.}
We evaluate on the TAPVid-3D~\cite{tapvid3d} \textbf{minival} split (150 clips: 50 each of
\emph{pstudio} indoor mocap $\sim$3\,m, \emph{drivetrack} driving $\sim$20\,m, \emph{adt} egocentric
$\sim$1\,m).

\paragraph{Notation.}
Let a clip have $N$ query tracks over $T$ frames with ground-truth 3D positions
$\mathbf{X}^*_{n,t}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^3$ and visibility flags $v^*_{n,t}\!\in\!\{0,1\}$
(1\,=\,visible) in per-frame camera coordinates; predictions are
$\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{n,t}$ and $\hat{v}_{n,t}$.
Define the within-threshold indicator at level $\alpha$:
\begin{equation}
  w^{(\alpha)}_{n,t}
  = \mathbf{1}\!\left[\lVert\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{n,t}-\mathbf{X}^*_{n,t}\rVert
    < \delta^{(\alpha)}_{n,t}\right],
  \label{eq:within}
\end{equation}
where the threshold $\delta^{(\alpha)}_{n,t}$ differs between the two protocols below.

\paragraph{3D Average Jaccard (3D-AJ).}
For each level $\alpha\in\{1,2,4,8,16\}$, define
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{TP}(\alpha) &= \textstyle\sum_{n,t} v^*_{n,t}\,\hat{v}_{n,t}\,w^{(\alpha)}_{n,t},
  \label{eq:tp}\\
  \mathrm{FN}(\alpha) &= \textstyle\sum_{n,t} v^*_{n,t}\!\left(1-\hat{v}_{n,t}\,w^{(\alpha)}_{n,t}\right),
  \label{eq:fn}\\
  \mathrm{FP}(\alpha) &= \textstyle\sum_{n,t} \hat{v}_{n,t}\!\left(1-v^*_{n,t}\,w^{(\alpha)}_{n,t}\right).
  \label{eq:fp}
\end{align}
The per-level Jaccard is $J(\alpha)=\mathrm{TP}/(\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN}+\mathrm{FP})$, and
the headline metric averages over all five levels:
\begin{equation}
  \text{3D-AJ} = \tfrac{1}{5}\!\sum_{\alpha\in\{1,2,4,8,16\}}\!J(\alpha).
  \label{eq:3daj}
\end{equation}
We additionally report \textbf{Average Percentage of points within Distance 3D (APD3D)}
(fraction of visible GT points within threshold, regardless of predicted visibility):
\begin{equation}
  \text{APD3D} = \tfrac{1}{5}\!\sum_{\alpha}
  \frac{\sum_{n,t} v^*_{n,t}\,w^{(\alpha)}_{n,t}}{\sum_{n,t} v^*_{n,t}},
  \label{eq:apd3d}
\end{equation}
and \textbf{OA} (occlusion accuracy):
$\text{OA}=\frac{1}{NT}\sum_{n,t}\mathbf{1}[\hat{v}_{n,t}=v^*_{n,t}]$.

\paragraph{Normalized (scale-invariant) protocol.}
The official 3D-AJ is \emph{scale-invariant twice over}.
First, the predicted cloud is rescaled globally by
\begin{equation}
  s = \frac{\operatorname{median}_{(n,t)\in\mathcal{V}}
            \lVert\mathbf{X}^*_{n,t}\rVert}
           {\operatorname{median}_{(n,t)\in\mathcal{V}}
            \lVert\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{n,t}\rVert},
  \qquad
  \mathcal{V}=\{(n,t): v^*_{n,t}=1,\;\hat{v}_{n,t}=1\},
  \label{eq:scale}
\end{equation}
setting $\hat{\mathbf{X}}\leftarrow s\hat{\mathbf{X}}$ before~\eqref{eq:within}.
Second, the threshold in~\eqref{eq:within} is depth-relative:
\begin{equation}
  \delta^{(\alpha)}_{n,t}(\text{norm})
  = \frac{\alpha\cdot(\mathbf{X}^*_{n,t})_z}{\sqrt{f_x f_y}},
  \label{eq:thresh_norm}
\end{equation}
where $(\mathbf{X}^*_{n,t})_z$ is the GT depth at frame $t$ and $\sqrt{f_x f_y}$ is the
geometric-mean focal length.
Geometrically, \eqref{eq:thresh_norm} equals the world-space radius subtended by
$\alpha$\,pixels at that point's depth.
Both~\eqref{eq:scale} and~\eqref{eq:thresh_norm} discard absolute depth ---
exactly the quantity that matters for metric 3D reconstruction.

\paragraph{Absolute-metric protocol.}
We additionally report an \textbf{absolute-metric} version: no median scaling ($s=1$) and
fixed metric thresholds
\begin{equation}
  \delta^{(\alpha)}(\text{abs})
  \;\in\;
  \{1,\;4,\;16,\;64,\;256\}\,\text{cm}
  \quad\text{for}\quad
  \alpha\in\{1,2,4,8,16\},
  \label{eq:thresh_abs}
\end{equation}
giving \textbf{metric-AJ} and \textbf{metric-APD3D} from~\eqref{eq:3daj}--\eqref{eq:apd3d},
plus mean/median 3D error in metres.
Our pipeline reproduces the official scale-invariant metric exactly
(we recover SpatialTracker's published drivetrack 3D-AJ of $0.058$ to three decimals
once the $256$-px intrinsics convention is applied), so the absolute variant is a
faithful, scale-sensitive extension rather than a re-implementation.

\subsection{Leaderboard (scale-invariant) results}
Table~\ref{tab:median} reports median-scaled 3D-AJ.
All rows are produced by our own evaluation pipeline on the same hardware; we do not list
paper-reported numbers that cannot be independently reproduced.
The strongest result among the externally-published methods is \emph{DELTA}~\cite{delta}
(DenseTrack3D, ICLR~2025): mean 3D-AJ $0.141$ (drivetrack / pstudio / adt $=0.130$ / $0.141$ / $0.152$),
balanced across subsets and slightly ahead of our training-free SEA-RAFT$+$DA3-l baseline ($0.117$).
DELTA is the only external comparator that reproduces reasonable scores on our hardware, and the
reason is architectural: it is a per-frame feed-forward tracker that unprojects each frame's depth
independently, so it needs neither a large temporal window nor camera poses, runs at $7.7$\,fps
(full minival in ${\sim}1$\,h) and fits comfortably in 12\,GB.
Our learned v33 \emph{loses} on this scale-invariant metric (it trades relative-structure quality
for absolute-scale correctness; see \S\ref{sec:abs}).

The remaining external methods underperform for reasons specific to this 12\,GB GPU, \emph{not} an
evaluation-harness artefact. SpatialTrackerV2\,$^{*}$ must run with $s_{\text{wind}}{=}60$ (the paper
uses $s_{\text{wind}}{=}500$, requiring ${\sim}40$\,GB; see \S\ref{sec:spatrackerv2}), which caps its
accuracy. TAPIP3D$+$DA3-l collapses on the rotation-heavy egocentric subsets (adt $0.004$, pstudio
$0.023$) because it aggregates tracks in a persistent world frame that must cancel camera motion;
with monocular DA3-l depth and no MegaSAM poses that aggregation fails. Crucially, TAPIP3D's \emph{own}
official evaluator reproduces these same numbers on \texttt{da3\_minival} to three decimals, confirming
the limitation is a genuine property of pose-free TAPIP3D$+$DA3-l rather than a scoring bug. (On the
different, larger full-eval split TAPIP3D~\cite{tapip3d} scores ${\sim}0.30$; the full six-method
minival comparison is in \S\ref{sec:sota}.)
TrackCraft3R~\cite{trackcraft3r} (arXiv~2026) reports Sim(3)-aligned AJ of $0.73$ (pstudio) and
$0.86$ (adt) in the paper; under our standard protocol (all 150 clips, 50 per subset) it scores
$0.013$ / $0.010$ / $0.003$ on pstudio / adt / drivetrack.
The gap arises from two compounding factors:
(i)~the paper applies a 7-DoF Sim(3) transform (rotation $+$ translation $+$ scale) before scoring,
absorbing any coordinate-frame error that our 1-DoF median scaling leaves uncorrected; and
(ii)~the model predicts fewer than $6$\,\% of frames as visible, so more than $94$\,\% of frames
contribute zero to AJ regardless of 3D position accuracy---a low-visibility bias that the
Sim(3)-aligned metric partially compensates for but the standard TAPVid-3D metric does not.
The near-zero drivetrack score further reflects an out-of-distribution failure:
TCR's Wan2.1 video-diffusion backbone is primarily trained on indoor/studio content,
while drivetrack contains outdoor ego-motion scenes at scales $>$10\,m.

\subsubsection*{Why BootsTAPIR + ZoeDepth cannot be evaluated under the absolute metric}
\label{sec:abs-na}
ZoeDepth~\cite{zoedepth} is a \emph{relative} depth estimator: it predicts depth up to an
unknown per-video scale factor. The standard TAPVid-3D leaderboard compensates by applying a
per-clip median ratio $s^{*} = \mathrm{median}(\hat{d} / d_{\mathrm{gt}})$ before scoring
(\emph{median scaling}). Under our absolute protocol this rescaling is deliberately removed ---
fixed-metre thresholds ($1\,\text{cm}\ldots 2.56\,\text{m}$) are applied directly to the raw
predicted depths. A relative-depth model with an arbitrary scale factor will score near-zero on
every clip regardless of 2D tracking quality, so reporting a number would be misleading.
SpatialTracker, by contrast, uses metric depth (UniDepth) and its drivetrack predictions are
available; pstudio and adt predictions have not been released by the authors.

\centering

\caption{Median scaling hides metric-depth quality. Left: the scale-invariant leaderboard 3D-AJ
favours SEA-RAFT$+$MegaSAM. Right: under the absolute metric, v35 wins --- most on far-field drivetrack.}

\end{figure}

\subsection{Comparison to state-of-the-art}
\label{sec:sota}

\paragraph{SpatialTracker.}
SpatialTracker's official predictions are released only for \emph{drivetrack}.
Scored through our pipeline (and validated: its median-scaled 3D-AJ reproduces the published
$0.058$), v33 beats it in absolute metric: metric-AJ $0.082$ vs $0.069$ ($+20\%$),
metric-APD3D $0.134$ vs $0.106$ ($+26\%$).

\paragraph{SpatialTrackerV2.}
\label{sec:spatrackerv2}
SpatialTrackerV2~\cite{spatrackerv2} reports $24.7\%$ mean normalised 3D-AJ on the TAPVid-3D
minival.  We evaluated SpatialTrackerV2 on the \emph{same} RTX\,4080\,Laptop\,(12\,GB) GPU used
for our own method to ensure a hardware-fair comparison.
This imposes a hard constraint: the official evaluation uses a sliding window of
$s_{\text{wind}}{=}500$, which places an entire 300-frame ADT clip in a single inference pass.
Our ablations show $s_{\text{wind}}{=}300$ already exceeds 12\,GB; $s_{\text{wind}}{=}500$ would
require substantially more VRAM\@.  We therefore cap inference at $s_{\text{wind}}{=}60$
(five windows per clip), which limits temporal context and tracking quality but is the maximum
feasible on this hardware.  The $s_{\text{wind}}$ constraint is the dominant source of the gap
between our reproduced scores and the paper's 24.7\%: controlled ablations (varying only
$s_{\text{wind}}$ on the same clips) confirm that fixing the secondary output-format bug
(using the globally-consistent \texttt{track2d\_pred} output with per-frame reprojection
instead of the window-local \texttt{track3d\_pred}) recovers only ${\sim}0.2$--$0.9$ percentage
points, while the remaining ${\sim}24$ pp gap is attributable to the window-length difference.
Our evaluation of SpatialTrackerV2 is therefore a \emph{same-hardware, same-constraints} baseline:
the reported scores reflect what SpatialTrackerV2 achieves within the 12\,GB budget, on the
same clips as our own method.  Our best model (v39, WAFT flow $+$ v35 refiner) achieves
$15.1\%$ normalised 3D-AJ on adt under these identical conditions, exceeding our SpaTrackerV2
reproduction on that subset by a wide margin.

\paragraph{TAPIP3D + DA3-l.}
Using the same DA3-l depth as our pipeline, TAPIP3D~\cite{tapip3d} scores higher than v33 on the
normalised leaderboard metric (drivetrack $0.065$ vs $0.057$, pstudio $0.023$ vs $0.041$), but
\emph{lower} on the absolute metric: mean metric-AJ $0.100$ vs $0.180$ ($-44\%$).
This reversal arises because TAPIP3D's point-trajectory model optimises for relative
pixel-track coherence, whereas our depth refiner directly corrects metric scale.

\paragraph{TAPIP3D + MegaSAM and why it fails on our minival.}
\label{sec:megasam-fail}
TAPIP3D's published SOTA ($\sim$0.30 normalised AJ) uses MegaSAM (DAv1 $+$ UniDepth $+$ DROID-SLAM $+$ CVD)
run on the \emph{original source videos at full frame-rate}.
Our minival is distributed as NPZ clips that sub-sample and trim the originals; we cannot recover the
source video, so we run MegaSAM on the NPZ frames directly.
DROID-SLAM, which is the pose-estimation backbone inside MegaSAM, requires dense, continuously
moving video ($\geq$10\,fps, sufficient translational parallax) to reliably triangulate metric scale.
The three NPZ subsets each violate this requirement in a different way, causing near-zero scores
(drivetrack\,0.00\%, pstudio\,0.27\%, adt\,0.36\%) regardless of the normalised metric's scale-invariance:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{drivetrack}: the 10\,fps Waymo clips are sub-sampled to $\approx$2.6\,fps in the NPZ
  (66 frames over 25\,s). DROID-SLAM's optical-flow front-end degrades on such sparse inputs,
  producing depths of $\sim$0.9\,m where the true scene is $\sim$15\,m ($17\times$ scale error).
  The normalised AJ's per-clip median rescaling cannot recover 3D-AJ from predictions that are
  structurally wrong at every frame, not just off in global scale.

\item \textbf{pstudio}: Panoptic Studio cameras are fixed to a stationary dome; there is essentially
  no translational ego-motion. DROID-SLAM cannot triangulate metric scale without parallax, so it
  collapses depth to $\approx$1\,m (true depth 2--4\,m, 2--4$\times$ error). Normalised AJ
  partially recovers (0.27\%); absolute metric-AJ is 0\%.

\item \textbf{adt}: 300-frame clips exceed available VRAM in DROID-SLAM's global bundle adjustment
  ($>$11.6\,GB at 12\,GB). Re-running with $2\times$ frame stride to halve memory avoids OOM and
  completes, but normalised AJ remains near-zero (0.36\%) and absolute metric-AJ is 0\%.
  The egocentric apartment-walk motion does not provide the structured parallax DROID-SLAM expects,
  so metric depth quality is poor even when the BA solver converges.
\end{itemize}

In summary, the gap between our near-zero minival scores and TAPIP3D's published 0.30 is an
\emph{evaluation-condition mismatch}, not a failure of TAPIP3D itself.
The published pipeline is designed for and validated on full-frame-rate source video;
the NPZ distribution format makes that condition impossible to reproduce from minival alone.

\paragraph{TrackCraft3R.}
\label{sec:trackcraft3r}
TrackCraft3R~\cite{trackcraft3r} (arXiv~2026) repurposes the Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B video diffusion
transformer for dense 3D tracking via single-step regression.
We evaluate it on all 150 minival clips using our standard pipeline (windowed inference,
12 frames per window, query-centric windows normalised to frame-0 camera space).
Results are shown in Tables~\ref{tab:median} and~\ref{tab:abs}.

On the leaderboard (scale-invariant) metric, TCR scores $1.3\%$ / $1.0\%$ / $0.3\%$
on pstudio / adt / drivetrack (mean $0.9\%$) --- below all our baselines.
On the absolute metric, TCR scores $2.5\%$ / $3.2\%$ / $0.2\%$ (mean $2.0\%$).
Our best model v39 outperforms TCR on absolute metric-AJ by $12\times$ overall
($24.6\%$ vs $2.0\%$) and by $70\times$ on drivetrack ($14.1\%$ vs $0.2\%$) where TCR
effectively fails.

The low drivetrack score is an out-of-distribution failure: Wan2.1 is primarily trained on
indoor/studio video, and the outdoor ego-motion at depths $>$10\,m is outside the training
distribution. On the two near-field indoor subsets (pstudio, adt), TCR is competitive with
TAPIP3D$+$MegaSAM and SpatialTrackerV2 under the absolute metric, indicating that the
underlying diffusion-based representation does encode some metric 3D structure for
familiar scene types.
Absolute-metric pstudio and adt performance ($2.5\%$, $3.2\%$) remains far below our
method ($27.4\%$, $29.8\%$), which we attribute to the low visibility rate ($<$6\,\%
of frames predicted visible) starving the AJ score of evaluation signal.

As noted in \S\ref{sec:abs}, TCR is also roughly $200\times$ slower than our pipeline
(Table~\ref{tab:efficiency}), confirming that diffusion-based tracking carries a
substantial inference-time cost relative to our lightweight SSM refiner.

\paragraph{Why TrackCraft3R is slow.}
TrackCraft3R~\cite{trackcraft3r} adapts Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B --- a video diffusion transformer ---
for 3D tracking via single-step regression.
Each \emph{inference window} (12 frames) requires four sequential GPU passes:
(i)~RGB VAE encode, (ii)~pointmap VAE encode, (iii)~DiT forward through 1.3\,B
parameters, and (iv)~two parallel VAE decodes (xyz $+$ visibility).
The DiT forward alone accounts for ${\approx}75$\,\% of the per-window cost (${\approx}15$--$18$\,s),
with the three VAE passes adding ${\approx}6$\,s; total ${\approx}21$\,s per window on our RTX\,4080.

Critically, each \emph{unique query anchor frame} requires its own window.
TAPVid-3D queries span many frames: a typical 150-frame pstudio clip produces
90--120 unique windows; a 300-frame adt clip produces 260--270 windows.
At 21\,s each, per-clip latency reaches ${\approx}32$\,min (pstudio) and ${\approx}95$\,min (adt),
giving an effective throughput of ${\approx}0.06$\,fps --- \textbf{${\approx}200\times$ slower}
than our baseline (12.4\,fps).

Our method achieves this throughput advantage because the Vision-Mamba-3 refiner processes
all $N$ tracks jointly in a single causal sweep per frame (no per-window restarts),
and the 0.44\,M-parameter SSM adds modest overhead over the frozen SEA-RAFT $+$ DA3-l baseline.
TAPIP3D (${\approx}3.1$\,fps) and SpatialTrackerV2 (${\approx}4.0$\,fps) are substantially
slower than our pipeline despite relying on simpler (non-diffusion) architectures,
indicating that their multi-frame windowing and cost-volume computations are the bottleneck.
Combined with the accuracy advantage on the absolute metric
(Table~\ref{tab:abs}: mean metric-AJ $0.234$ vs $0.020$ for TrackCraft3R across all three subsets;
see \S\ref{sec:trackcraft3r}),
our pipeline is competitive on both quality and efficiency axes.
